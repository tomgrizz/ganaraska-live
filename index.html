How the model works

Every frame from the counter's underwater tunnel camera runs through the same four steps — find the fish, read it, name it, and follow it. No infrared beam, no fixed clip length: the model decides when a fish is there and captures the whole passage.

1 DETECT
Find the fish
An object detector scans every frame and draws a box around anything fish-shaped.
2 CROP
Cut it out
The fish is cropped out on its own and shrunk to a small square, so the identifier sees only that fish — not the tunnel walls or another fish swimming past.
3 IDENTIFY
Read & name it
A DINOv3 vision model turns the crop into a 768-number “fingerprint” and names the species with a confidence.
4 TRACK
Follow & count
A tracker follows each fish across frames, so it’s counted once and its direction — up or downstream — is resolved.

How it fits into the counter

The model runs alongside the existing Vaki counter, reading only the live camera view. It never touches the infrared scanner or the official recordings, so nothing it does can affect the count. Running the two in parallel is exactly how we test the model against the scanner-and-clip method.

The feature space — drag to rotate

DINOv3 maps each fish into a 768-dimension “fingerprint” space where same-species fish land together. Above are 1,000 real fish from the validation set, coloured by species — that 768-D space squeezed down to 3 so you can spin it around. The clean clusters are why the model can tell them apart; notice Chinook and Coho, the trickiest pair, sitting closest.

How it was made

The vision model, DINOv3, was pre-trained on 1.7 billion images, so it already understands shape, colour and texture. We then fine-tuned it on thousands of fish crops that technicians had labelled by species, across both rivers and several seasons.

  • Detector — a DETR model finds the fish in each frame.
  • Classifier — DINOv3 ViT-B/16 names the species from the crop.
  • Tracker — BoT-SORT links a fish across frames to count it once and read its direction.
  • Accuracy — about 98% species agreement on held-out fish it never trained on.

A research tool, not an official count. The “Ganaraska River” caption in the clips is burned in by the camera itself.
